Custom Water Bottle MOQ and Pricing: What Changes the Quote

A wholesale sourcing guide to stock versus custom minimums, printing setup, colors, cartons and landed cost.

A wholesale sourcing guide to stock versus custom minimums, printing setup, colors, cartons and landed cost. Use the practical sourcing criteria below to define a quote, verify samples and avoid unapproved assumptions about a bottle model.

Before requesting a quote
  • Request separate blank-stock and custom quotes
  • Ask about MOQ per color and print artwork
  • Split tooling/setup from recurring unit price

Separate stock orders from fully customized production

Existing unprinted bottles, bottles in standard colors and completely new designs involve different setup demands. Minimums may change for custom resin color, dedicated molds, print positions or gift packaging. Ask the supplier which part of the order controls MOQ, rather than asking for one factory-wide number. A trial quantity can be feasible for one model but not for a newly molded body.

Ask for quantity-break pricing with identical specifications

Request comparable quotes for target order levels, such as your trial quantity, next reorder level and annual volume if known. Hold the model, resin grade, cap, printing method and shipping term constant across those tiers. Separate one-time setup or mold charges from recurring unit costs. This helps buyers see whether a larger quantity actually reduces total spending or only shifts stock risk.

Understand decoration setup and color minimums

A one-color logo on an existing flat surface may need less setup than complex wraparound multi-color decoration. Supplier minimums can apply per body color, cap color, artwork version or retail packaging design. A mixed-color order is not necessarily a single manufacturing lot. Ask about setup fees, print rejection allowances and how unused materials will be handled when quantities change.

Consider the full landed cost

Unit cost alone omits cartons, inserts, master case dimensions, freight, customs and inspection expenses. Estimate landed cost per sellable unit for the intended delivery destination. Bulky jugs and lightweight compact bottles can differ greatly in shipping efficiency. Where shipment terms differ, compare them on a common basis before choosing a supplier.

Balance first order risk against reorder continuity

For a new product line, validate colors and channel demand before committing to every possible design. Ask whether stock colors can support a pilot and when branded production makes sense. Once a model performs, agree on repeat-order artwork revision, material consistency, spare parts and replenishment scheduling. A slightly higher pilot price may be acceptable if it lowers unsold inventory exposure.

Send the information necessary for a firm quote

State the model and capacity, body and lid materials, colors per design, print artwork, pack method, destination, expected quantities and launch deadline. Ask which costs are fixed and which change by volume. A transparent quote should state assumptions rather than presenting an apparently precise price before all customization choices are known.

Buyer checklist

Use these points when comparing a factory sample, artwork approval and purchase quotation.

  • Request separate blank-stock and custom quotes
  • Ask about MOQ per color and print artwork
  • Split tooling/setup from recurring unit price
  • Compare landed cost under the same Incoterm
  • Check lead time and minimum reorder quantities

Frequently asked questions

Is 100 pieces enough for a logo order?

It depends on model stock, decoration method, color and supplier setup. Do not rely on a universal MOQ; share the requested model and artwork for an accurate answer.

Why did a repeat quote increase despite higher quantity?

Resin grade, packaging, labor, freight term, exchange rates or print specifications may have changed. Compare line items and the approved spec before attributing the difference to quantity alone.
