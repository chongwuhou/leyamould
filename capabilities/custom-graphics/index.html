WHAT THIS SERVICE COVERS

From Idea to an Approved Specification

Color and surface design often determine whether a bottle looks like a recognizable retail collection. But a Pantone value, a molded pigment and a decorated gradient are different production choices. They need different materials, process checks, minimum order quantities and physical sampling; XUYI reviews them against the selected bottle shape and actual plastic substrate.

01

Plan molded color separately from artwork

When the bottle body color is made through resin coloring, the result is affected by material, wall thickness, transparency and mold texture. A visual gradient may instead depend on a specific compatible decoration method. A rendered design does not prove a particular model can be mass-produced with that effect. Agree on what is solid molded color and what is later printing.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Choose opaque, translucent or transparent
  • Specify body and lid colors separately
  • Clarify whether artwork is in-mold or applied afterward
02

Build a workable color palette

Provide Pantone targets or a physical color chip, while recognizing that digital RGB images rarely match molded resin exactly. Test colors under normal lighting on the actual bottle. Transparent bodies can alter the appearance of white, pastel and dark printed graphics. Specify which deviations are acceptable in volume production.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Select primary and alternate approved samples
  • Review print visibility on translucent bodies
  • Approve acceptable color variation
03

Understand solid, gradient and wraparound treatments

A solid pigment often depends on molding material and lot size; a gradient or wraparound pattern may require a verified transfer, screen/digital system or different finishing process. Complex continuous graphics can break around handles, ribs and tapered shapes. Request a production feasibility check with the bottle maker, particularly for PP and other difficult-to-print surfaces.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Map design across ribs and seams
  • Confirm method compatible with selected resin
  • Review minimum graphic details and registration
04

Surface texture changes appearance and handling

Matte and glossy tools create different look and tactile feel. Soft-feel coating or overmolded grip is a different component/process decision and cannot be promised just because an image depicts a textured bottle. Check scuff marks, fingerprint visibility, rub performance and cleaning conditions on approved samples.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Specify molded texture vs applied coating
  • Review grip zones and logo overlap
  • Check cleaning/rub behavior
05

Synchronize design with MOQ and repeat orders

A combination of body color, contrasting cap color, gradient design and full-color packaging may have multiple production minimums. Build a SKU matrix showing body, lid, graphic version and carton variation. Agree on a signed appearance reference and confirm how many colorways can be packed together for the delivery channel.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Create color-by-model SKU matrix
  • Separate mold-color and decoration setup fees
  • Keep final appearance reference for reorders

BUYER QUESTIONS

Color-Approval Questions Before Bulk Production

01

Can a photo's gradient be matched exactly?

A photo is a starting point. Final visual results depend on substrate, ink/process, lighting and approval tolerances. A decorated sample is required.

02

Can I order one different cap color for every bottle?

It depends on stock availability and minimum quantities for each separately molded component. Confirm the requested color matrix.

03

Does translucent mean the same thing as Tritan?

No. Translucency describes appearance; Tritan is a resin family. Multiple plastics can have different optical properties.

04

How should we approve color accuracy?

Use agreed color references plus actual samples under controlled viewing conditions; keep a signed sample for future orders.
