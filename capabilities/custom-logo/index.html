WHAT THIS SERVICE COVERS

From Idea to an Approved Specification

A logo that looks perfect on a flat PDF can distort on a tapered sports bottle or lose contrast on a translucent wall. We work from the actual bottle surface, resin grade, desired artwork and quantity to review suitable print or decoration options. Screen, pad, compatible transfers or other methods are considered case by case rather than promised for every polymer.

Key Decisions Before Production

01

Map the real logo area first

Request a printing-layout drawing or a photograph annotated with maximum width, height and distance from handles, ribs or seams. Fix the orientation of the logo relative to the spout and lid. A curved surface changes perceived proportions and may constrain multi-color registration. Separate bottle-body artwork from cap and retail-box artwork.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Provide a vector logo in AI, EPS or PDF
  • Specify the viewing position relative to the lid
  • Confirm minimum line widths and safe margins
02

Compare the process instead of chasing buzzwords

Screen printing is useful for some single or limited-color designs; pad printing can address smaller or irregular areas where technically feasible. Transfer or digital processes may enable more complex artwork but demand compatible plastic, inks and curing. Laser processes developed for stainless tumblers are not automatically suitable for PP, Tritan, PC or PETG.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Confirm ink compatibility with the exact resin
  • Ask about color count and registration
  • Request a physical printed sample
03

Color management, white underprint and transparency

Brand colors appear different against opaque black, translucent pastel and transparent bodies. White ink backing or adjusted color separation may be necessary. Define Pantone or other references with a tolerance agreement, and evaluate the print on the actual molded-color sample under normal light rather than relying on monitor mockups.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Provide artwork and preferred color values
  • Test contrast on every important body color
  • Approve a realistic decorated physical sample
04

Print durability and food-contact boundaries

Normal handling, cleaning and rubbing can damage some decorations. Agree on an appropriate adhesion, rub and cleaning test and inspect the printed sample after testing. Confirm where printing sits relative to drinking-contact areas and retailer packaging claims. Do not advertise permanent, scratchproof or dishwasher-safe printing without evidence tied to the chosen process.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Keep decoration away from undesired contact areas
  • Agree test method and pass criteria
  • Control ink/process changes on repeat orders
05

Make the purchase order precise

Quote and approve logo size, print locations, ink colors, setup charges, unit print cost and packaging artwork as separate fields. Complex graphics and each SKU/color variation can change the minimum order. Keep revision IDs and a physical golden sample so a reorder does not drift from the original approved mark.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Record artwork revision and position
  • Ask MOQ per design/color combination
  • Approve packaging copy separately

BUYER QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I print full-color artwork on any plastic bottle?

Not universally. Feasibility depends on curved geometry, plastic resin, surface treatment, production method and run size.

Should my logo be supplied as a PNG?

PNG can show the intended visual result, but editable vector artwork generally provides more reliable production dimensions and separations.

Can the same printed artwork fit different bottle sizes?

Usually the design must be mapped separately to each printable area; size and curvature changes may require a new proof.

Is laser engraving standard for plastic bottles?

No. It is not a universal plastic decoration method and must be reviewed for the exact resin and part. Do not assume metal-bottle techniques transfer directly.
