WHAT THIS SERVICE COVERS

From Idea to an Approved Specification

A truly custom bottle is an engineering project, not a print job. The body-forming process, cap and handle construction, polymer behavior and tolerances affect the mold route. We can discuss feasibility through the suitable XUYI or manufacturing-partner production route; engineering capability, tooling costs and ownership are confirmed per project.

01

Define precisely what changes require tooling

A printed logo and standard body color usually do not require a new cavity. New body geometry, an integrated handle, neck thread or cap shape may. Mark whether the requested change affects appearance only or water sealing and mechanical function. The second class needs more careful fit, assembly and durability evaluation.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Draw body and neck dimensions
  • List revised lid, cap, straw and gasket parts
  • Separate cosmetic changes from functional changes
02

Select appropriate body and component forming methods

Injection molding is commonly associated with caps, small handles and complex plastic components; plastic bottle bodies may use blow-molding or other processes depending on polymer, design and manufacturing route. The mold maker must verify resin suitability, wall thickness, draft, shrinkage and neck tolerances. Do not describe all bottles as injection-molded products.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Confirm resin and finished wall/weight targets
  • Review whether the body uses blowing or another suitable process
  • Ask for neck, cap and seal fit checks
03

Design-for-manufacture before cutting steel

Before committing to a mold, assess the 2D/3D part definition, parting line, surface finish, draft angles, undercuts, gate and ejector positions if relevant, and assembly interfaces. Evaluate where sinks, flash or deformation would be unacceptable. Review how changing the bottle capacity could affect mold size, carton dimensions and lid alignment.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Review engineering drawings and samples
  • Document critical sealing and thread tolerances
  • Agree visible surface and finish expectations
04

Prototypes, trial shots and acceptance

A visual 3D model cannot validate liquid containment or assembly quality. Where feasible, use prototypes to check handling and mechanical layout, then inspect molded trial samples for fit, appearance, dimensional stability, closure function and relevant material evidence. Decide defect acceptance, rework and pilot approval before production quantities are committed.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Review first samples with the actual cap/gasket
  • Record leakage or opening-force test conditions
  • Approve signed drawings and reference sample
05

Commercial responsibilities and mold ownership

A responsible quote separates design services, tooling charges, sample trials and ongoing per-unit costs. Clarify in writing ownership and storage of molds, maintenance responsibility, design-file access, revision allowances and transfer rights, when applicable. NDA, exclusivity and third-party supplier involvement should be agreed rather than implied.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Ask who owns and stores each tool
  • Agree redesign and rework responsibility
  • Separate tooling amortization from unit cost

TOOLING DECISION

Does Your Design Really Need a New Mold?

One of the most expensive purchasing mistakes is commissioning tooling for a change that existing components can support.

Requested changeLikely route to evaluateWhat the factory needs
Different printed logoStart from a standard bottleArtwork file, printable dimensions and surface/material details
Alternative lid / carry handleCheck existing component compatibility firstThread and gasket dimensions, assembly sample and test criteria
New body profile or unique closureEngineering review and possible new toolingCAD, material grade, volume target, tolerances and project budget
Integrated embossed brandMold-surface modification assessment3D lettering definition, rights and future mold reuse

Product models such as 2095 and 5340 show existing bottle forms. Their appearance alone does not establish the feasibility or cost of modifying a mold.

BUYER QUESTIONS

Questions to Resolve Before Paying a Tooling Deposit

01

Do custom bottles always need injection molding?

No. Caps and certain parts are commonly injection molded, while bottle bodies may use suitable blow-forming or other processes. The engineering route depends on resin and geometry.

02

Does a new printed logo require a new mold?

Normally not for compatible surface printing. An integrated embossed or debossed logo may require tooling changes.

03

Who owns the mold?

Ownership and use rights must be specified in the tooling contract and cannot be assumed from a one-time fee.

04

Can you quote a mold from a photograph?

A photograph supports initial screening, but accurate quotes require dimensions, part geometry, material and production targets.

AFTER MOLD DESIGN

Tool Trials Need a Production and Inspection Plan

Acceptance should cover neck fit, lid operation, wall quality and material—not just first-shot appearance. Put drawing control and sample testing in the tooling agreement before bulk production is booked.
