WHAT THIS SERVICE COVERS

From Idea to an Approved Specification

A wholesale bottle becomes a sellable SKU only when the physical product, accessories, labels and packing agree. Retail color boxes, sleeves, protective bags and bulk cartons have different costs and freight footprints. We develop packing requirements from the selected plastic bottle and destination market rather than assuming one generic box works for every cap or handle.

01

Choose retail-ready or bulk-first pack formats

Simple individual bags or sleeves may be practical for promotional orders, while branded folding cartons and gift packs support retail shelves. Bottle shape, cap height, handles, mixer inserts and straps can change the interior footprint. Decide whether the consumer expects a single bottle, bundled accessories or a multipack, then specify the contents precisely.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Confirm bottle and accessories per sales unit
  • Identify retail versus wholesale distribution
  • Choose simple protection or branded presentation
02

Design the protective structure around a real sample

Transparent plastic surfaces and printed logos can scuff during transit if items touch. Paper wrap, inserts, dividers or compartment arrangements may be considered, subject to cost and packaging sustainability targets. Approve how the lid is secured and any loose accessories are retained. Do not use stock packaging pictures as proof of the proposed shipping strength.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Measure packed bottle including lid/handle
  • Decide insert, divider or sleeve needs
  • Inspect packaging abrasion on samples
03

Finish carton engineering with measured data

Request units per carton, carton external dimensions, gross/net weight and handling or pallet information where applicable. Large jugs and compact sports bottles have very different freight volume per unit. Compare shipping terms and landed cost using real carton configurations. Carton compression or transit test methods are chosen for the actual route and retailer needs.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Approve units/carton and outer dimensions
  • Confirm gross weight and carton marking
  • Validate stacking and transport conditions
04

Prepare artwork and legally appropriate labels

Print-ready artwork should identify dielines, bleed/safe areas, barcodes, warnings, capacity, market language and any importer details required by the buyer's destination. Do not carry over group-site claims such as universal FDA/LFGB certification, 'eco' or BPA-free without model-specific evidence. The buyer should approve legal copy and symbol usage before packaging prints.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Provide vector art and barcode data
  • Confirm language, warnings and destination rules
  • Sign off carton design and revision
05

Control order assembly and shipping records

For mixed-color cartons, decide which SKU/color and how many go in every case. Agree whether product labels and cartons identify body and cap variants, whether serial/lot traceability is needed and who supplies shipping documents. Packing photos, carton counts and a final specification reduce disputes and customer returns.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Confirm SKU and color breakdown per carton
  • Check printed carton marks and pack list
  • Retain version control for reorders

BUYER QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Can custom packaging be ordered for a standard bottle?

Yes when packaging is feasible for the selected product. Minimum quantities and carton costs vary with print colors and dieline.

Can we mix bottle colors in a single master carton?

Sometimes, but the packing list, labels and production quantities need to specify the distribution clearly.

Who supplies barcode and destination warnings?

The buyer or its appointed regulatory representative should approve required local wording, barcode data and compliance claims.

Can packing reduce freight cost?

Improved carton utilization can help, but actual savings depend on carton dimensions, weight, transport mode and handling constraints.
