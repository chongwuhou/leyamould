WHAT THIS SERVICE COVERS

From Idea to an Approved Specification

Most projects can start with an established XUYI plastic bottle model, then refine the capacity, body and lid colors, decoration and retail presentation. A new bottle silhouette or unusual drinking mechanism takes a different engineering and tooling route. We help B2B buyers define that route before a final quotation or production commitment.

01

Start with the right bottle architecture

Sports bottles, children's straw bottles, shaker cups and large water jugs have different lids, carrying loads and cleaning requirements. Choose a product family and exact model first; identify whether the project changes decoration only or the component geometry. A 600 mL bottle body cannot automatically accept a larger closure or straw simply because the threads look similar.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Identify the product SKU and preferred volume
  • Confirm cap type, straw, seal and carry handle
  • Decide what can remain a standard stock component
02

Material decisions: PC, Tritan, PP and PETG

Body, lid, straw and gasket do not always share one polymer. Clarify intended water temperature, permitted cleaning conditions, transparency, finish and destination market. Conventional BPA-based PC should not be marketed as BPA-free without product-specific evidence. Resin grade, colorants and the final assembled product must be reviewed for any food-contact or BPA-free statement.

Buyer checkpoints
  • State whether BPA-free is a contractual requirement
  • Specify intended liquid, temperature and cleaning
  • Ask for component-level material and supporting evidence
03

Custom appearance without redesigning the bottle

Many private-label projects use existing molded bodies with a selected solid color, compatible logo print and buyer-designed packaging. Gradients, translucent colors, multi-position graphics or different lid colors are possible only where compatible with the selected resin and manufacturing route. Custom color minimums may apply independently to lids and bodies.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Provide a color reference or Pantone target
  • Ask which decoration area is actually printable
  • Confirm color minimums and logo setup fees
04

Validate the sample as a complete retail SKU

Approve the actual bottle plus lid, straw or mixer insert, packing components and graphics together. Review assembled appearance, seal placement, repeated opening, surface scuffs, print placement and the information required for your sales market. Images alone are not acceptance samples. Record the approved artwork version and component specification for repeat production.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Approve the precise body and lid combination
  • Keep a decorated reference sample
  • Specify packaging and product labeling
05

Create a comparable wholesale RFQ

Include quantity by color, model numbers, logo positions, packaging choices, target delivery date and destination. Ask the factory to separate unit costs, decoration/tooling setup, sample cost, test requests and packing requirements. MOQ and lead time must be quoted for the final variant, not inferred from a general 'custom water bottle' claim.

Buyer checkpoints
  • Request quote by SKU and quantity tier
  • Define destination and shipping term
  • Document any material or design exclusions

BUYER QUESTIONS

Private-Label Decisions, Answered

Can we customize an existing bottle without a new mold?

Often, provided the requested colors, logo and pack work with the selected stock design. A new bottle structure, embossed graphic or closure may require tooling.

Which plastic is best for BPA-free bottles?

No material name alone proves a whole assembled bottle is BPA-free. Tritan, PP and PETG are options to assess; request product-specific statements and applicable test evidence.

Will the sample match the mass-production order?

The sample should be approved against a controlled bill of materials, decoration file and pack specification. Changes require review before production.

How do we get an accurate wholesale price?

Send the chosen model, variant details, quantity tiers, artwork, destination and packing requirement. Pricing and MOQ depend on these details.
